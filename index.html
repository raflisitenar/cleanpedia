Clean by surface,
not by guesswork.

Practical cleaning guidance for ordinary homes. Start with what happened, identify the material you are cleaning, then choose the gentlest reasonable method before escalating.

The basic rule

Always check the manufacturer's care instructions first. Test unfamiliar methods in an inconspicuous area, especially on delicate, coated, dyed or natural materials.

    02 / SURFACE LIBRARY

    The material changes the method.

    “Clean it” is incomplete advice. Water tolerance, finish, porosity and manufacturer instructions can all change what is appropriate.

    SURFACE 01

    Painted walls

    Start dry where possible. For washable finishes, use minimal moisture and gentle pressure after checking paint-care guidance.

    • Avoid aggressive scrubbing on delicate finishes.
    • Test before treating a visible area.
    SURFACE 02

    Finished wood

    Dust with a soft material and avoid leaving excess moisture on the surface. Use products compatible with the specific finish.

    • Do not assume all wood finishes tolerate the same cleaner.
    • Wipe spills promptly when safe to do so.
    SURFACE 03

    Glass & mirrors

    Remove loose dust first, then use an appropriate glass-cleaning method with a clean, low-lint cloth.

    • Avoid flooding edges or frames.
    • Check coatings and specialty glass instructions.
    SURFACE 04

    Stainless steel

    Use a non-abrasive approach and wipe with the grain where visible. Product-specific appliance instructions take priority.

    • Avoid abrasive pads that may scratch.
    • Drying can reduce visible water marks.
    SURFACE 05

    Upholstery

    Vacuum loose debris with a suitable attachment and identify the fabric's care instructions before adding moisture or cleaning product.

    • Hidden areas are useful for compatibility testing.
    • Some upholstery requires professional methods.
    SURFACE 06

    Natural stone

    Stone types and sealers vary. Follow stone or installer guidance and avoid assuming a general household acid or cleaner is compatible.

    • Acid-sensitive stone can be damaged by unsuitable products.
    • Blot spills rather than spreading them.

    04 / CLEANING RHYTHM

    A routine is easier than a rescue mission.

    Frequency depends on household size, pets, allergies, climate, material and use. Treat this as a planning framework rather than a universal schedule.

    After use

    • Address fresh spills
    • Reset food-prep surfaces
    • Hang or dry wet items appropriately

    Frequent

    • High-use surfaces
    • Visible crumbs and soil
    • Kitchen and bathroom touchpoints as needed

    Weekly rhythm

    • Floor care
    • Dusting accessible areas
    • Bedding or textile tasks as appropriate

    Periodic

    • Under/behind movable items
    • Vents and less-visible dust areas
    • Manufacturer-recommended appliance care
    05 / SAFETY FIRST

    Cleaning chemistry is not a mixing experiment.

    Do not casually mix products.

    Some combinations can release dangerous gases or cause harmful reactions. Follow product labels and never combine cleaners unless the manufacturer explicitly instructs it.

    Ventilation matters.

    Use products according to label ventilation requirements and avoid prolonged exposure to fumes or aerosols.

    Store responsibly.

    Keep cleaning products in their original labeled containers and away from children, pets, food and incompatible materials.

    06 / GLOSSARY

    Cleaning terms in plain language.

    AbrasiveA material or product that cleans partly through friction and may scratch surfaces that are not compatible with it.
    BlotPressing an absorbent material onto a spill or mark to lift moisture without aggressively rubbing it outward.
    DisinfectUsing an appropriate registered or approved process/product to reduce or inactivate specified microorganisms according to label directions. Cleaning and disinfecting are not identical tasks.
    PorousA material with openings or structure that can absorb liquid, affecting how stains and cleaning methods behave.
    Contact timeThe period a disinfecting product must remain on a surface as specified by its label to work as intended.
    Spot testTrying a method on a small inconspicuous area first to check for discoloration, damage or other unwanted effects.
    07 / FAQ

    Common home-cleaning questions.

    Should I clean from top to bottom?

    Often, yes. Dust or debris from higher surfaces can fall onto lower ones, so cleaning higher areas before floors can reduce repeated work. The best order still depends on the room and task.

    Is more cleaning product better?

    Not necessarily. Excess product can leave residue, be harder to rinse or wipe away, and may be inappropriate for the material. Follow the product label rather than increasing the amount by guesswork.

    Can vinegar be used on every surface?

    No. Acidic products can be unsuitable for some natural stone, finishes, grout situations, appliances and other materials. Check manufacturer or material-specific guidance first.

    What is the difference between cleaning and disinfecting?

    Cleaning removes soil and material from a surface. Disinfection uses an appropriate product or process against specified microorganisms and depends on directions such as concentration, application and contact time. Not every household surface needs routine disinfection.

    How should I deal with an unknown stain?

    Avoid aggressive treatment until you know more about the surface and likely source. Remove loose material gently, limit spreading, check care instructions and test any proposed method in an inconspicuous area.

    When should I use a professional cleaner?

    Professional help can make sense for valuable or delicate materials, persistent contamination, large areas, hazardous substances, mold problems requiring specialist assessment, or when manufacturer guidance specifies professional care.
